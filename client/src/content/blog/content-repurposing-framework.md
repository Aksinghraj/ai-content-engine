Publishing consistently does not require inventing ten unrelated ideas every week. It requires learning how to find the useful angles already present in one strong source piece.

This is content repurposing at its best: not copying and pasting the same thought, but translating the idea into formats that give different audiences a practical reason to pay attention.

## Start with a source worth expanding

Choose a video, podcast, walkthrough, or long-form explanation that has a clear promise. A ten-minute video about building a repeatable content workflow, for example, contains more than one post. It contains a problem, a method, examples, objections, and lessons learned.

Before creating anything else, write down the source's central idea in one sentence. This prevents the repurposed posts from drifting into disconnected advice.

## Find the ten angles

One useful framework is to extract a different job from the same source material:

1. A short hook that names the problem.
2. A quick lesson that explains the first step.
3. A myth-versus-reality post.
4. A checklist.
5. A customer or founder perspective.
6. A quote card or memorable line.
7. A before-and-after scenario.
8. A tactical carousel or thread outline.
9. A question that invites the audience to share their experience.
10. A clear call to action for people who want the full method.

The point is not to force every source into exactly ten posts. The point is to look for multiple uses before you move on to the next idea.

## Adapt the structure, not just the length

Different platforms reward different reading habits. A LinkedIn post can lead with a point of view and build an argument. An Instagram carousel needs scannable steps. An X post often needs a single strong observation. A video clip needs a moment that works without the rest of the recording.

Repurposing should therefore preserve the core idea while changing the opening, pacing, and call to action for the format in front of you.
