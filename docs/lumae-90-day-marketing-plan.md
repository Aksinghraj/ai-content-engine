# Lumae AI: 90-Day Marketing Plan

**Prepared for:** Veer Rajput, Lumae AI  
**Planning horizon:** 90 days from launch of this plan  
**Primary objective:** Turn Lumae’s promise—one idea, ready for every channel—into a repeatable path from qualified visitor to activated free user and, later, a confident Pro customer.

## 1. Executive Direction

Lumae should not market itself as another generic AI writer. Its strongest position is a **creator workflow system**: a creator starts with one idea, adapts it into channel-ready content, keeps the work connected, and moves it toward publishing. The marketing must therefore demonstrate a visible before-and-after transformation rather than merely listing AI features.

> **Positioning statement:** Lumae helps independent creators and small teams turn one clear idea into channel-ready posts, scripts, captions, and publishing plans—without losing their voice or rebuilding the work from scratch.

The first 90 days should prioritize **product-led growth, founder-led education, and credible proof**. The goal is not immediate mass reach. The goal is to learn which audience, promise, and activation path produce repeatable use.

The creator economy is a relevant growth environment: IAB projected U.S. creator ad spend at **$37 billion in 2025**, up **26% year-over-year**, and reported that creators are now considered a “must buy” channel by 48% of creator-ad buyers.[1] Lumae should use this trend as context, but earn attention through useful content and transparent product demonstrations—not trend claims alone.

## 2. Important Product-Messaging Decision Before Promotion

The public home page currently says **“Three free generations”** while the pricing page says **“5 generations per day.”** Resolve this before paid promotion, creator outreach, or launch posts. Every public channel must use one verified free-tier promise, one free-to-Pro comparison, and one upgrade trigger.

| Decision | Recommended standard |
|---|---|
| Free offer | Use the product-confirmed entitlement only after the website copy is unified. |
| Primary CTA | “Create your first channel-ready content package.” |
| Activation moment | A new user completes one meaningful output and saves, copies, schedules, or repurposes it. |
| Pro reason | More capacity, ongoing history, automation/scheduling, and advanced analytics—not vague “more AI.” |
| Trust promise | Secure sign-in, clear privacy controls, no false delivery claims, and transparent limitations. |

## 3. Priority Customer Segments

Lumae should start narrow. A single clear audience converts more reliably than broad “everyone who creates content” language.

| Segment | Core pain | Lumae job-to-be-done | Best first channel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solo service creators | They know their expertise but struggle to turn it into regular posts. | Turn one client insight into a week of platform-appropriate content. | Instagram Reels, LinkedIn, founder-led demos. |
| Early-stage business owners | Content is inconsistent and they cannot hire a full content team. | Build a practical content workflow from one product or customer problem. | LinkedIn, Instagram, business communities. |
| Freelance social-media managers | They repeatedly reformat ideas for multiple clients and channels. | Accelerate first drafts, formats, scripts, and approvals without losing context. | LinkedIn, creator partnerships, referrals. |

For the first 30 days, choose **one primary segment**. The recommended initial segment is **solo service creators and small business owners who already post but cannot sustain a workflow**. Their problem is concrete, their desired output is visible, and Lumae’s core workflow is easy to demonstrate.

## 4. Message Architecture

Every post, landing section, and demo should follow the same message sequence.

| Layer | Message |
|---|---|
| Problem | “You should not have to restart your thinking for every channel.” |
| Outcome | “Turn one idea into a useful content system.” |
| Mechanism | “Shape the idea into posts, scripts, captions, formats, and a plan.” |
| Proof | Show a real input, the resulting assets, and the creator’s edit choices. |
| CTA | “Create your first channel-ready package free.” |

Avoid phrases such as “viral guaranteed,” “fully automated growth,” or “replace your content team.” They weaken credibility and can create unsafe expectations. Prefer specific language: “first draft,” “channel-ready starting point,” “keep your voice in control,” and “review before publishing.”

## 5. Acquisition Strategy: Three Focused Growth Loops

### Loop A: Founder-Led Demonstrations

Publish short demonstrations from the founder account and Lumae account. Each demonstration should start with a familiar creator problem and end with one usable artifact. Do not lead with the interface; lead with the transformation.

Examples include: “One client question → LinkedIn post + Instagram carousel outline + 30-second Reel script,” “One product launch idea → 7-day content plan,” and “One rough voice note → refined caption options.”

Publish the same core lesson in native formats:

| Channel | Cadence | Native format | CTA |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Instagram | 4 short-form posts per week | Reels, carousel breakdowns, story polls | Save the idea or try the free workflow. |
| LinkedIn | 3 posts per week | Founder lessons, practical teardown, customer problem narrative | Comment with a workflow problem or start free. |
| YouTube Shorts | 2 posts per week | 30–60 second before/after demos | Visit the profile link for the full workflow. |
| Lumae Journal | 1 evergreen article per week | Searchable tutorial or framework | Build an email capture or free-workflow CTA into the article. |

### Loop B: Useful Templates and Lead Capture

Create one downloadable or in-product “starter system” every two weeks. A template must solve a small, real problem without requiring a paid plan.

Recommended first four assets are:

1. **30 hooks for local-service creators.**
2. **The one-idea-to-seven-posts weekly planner.**
3. **A carousel outline for turning a client question into authority content.**
4. **A solo-founder launch-week content checklist.**

Each asset should point to the same next step: import the idea into Lumae and build the first output. Use an email capture only when the file has clear standalone value; otherwise, use a direct free-account CTA to reduce friction.

### Loop C: Small Creator Partnerships

Do not begin with large influencers. Identify 15–25 micro-creators or freelancer educators whose audiences match the chosen segment. Offer a free Pro period or co-created workflow tutorial in exchange for honest product use and clear disclosure. Do not buy or fabricate reviews.

IAB reports that selecting suitable creators and measuring results remain key challenges for brands.[1] Lumae should therefore use a small tracked pilot: unique links, a single offer, a defined content brief, and a 14-day review. Continue only with partners who create authentic use cases and activated users.

## 6. Product-Led Funnel

The marketing funnel must join up with the product, not end at account creation.

| Funnel stage | User question | Lumae action | Core measurement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discover | “Why should I care?” | Demonstration, problem-led post, useful template. | Qualified landing-page visit. |
| Evaluate | “Will this help my workflow?” | Clear home page, targeted use-case page, pricing clarity. | CTA click and account-start rate. |
| Activate | “Can I make something useful now?” | Guided first generation with a concrete prompt/example. | First meaningful output within first session. |
| Adopt | “Can I rely on it next week?” | Saved history, repurposing, schedule/plan prompt. | Second active day within seven days. |
| Convert | “Why upgrade?” | Contextual upgrade at a genuine limit or advanced workflow moment. | Qualified free user to paid conversion. |
| Refer | “Would I share this?” | Shareable before/after output and creator-template collaboration. | Referral or share event. |

For internal planning, define a **Product-Qualified Lead (PQL)** as a free user who completes at least two meaningful outputs and either saves/repurposes content, reaches a free-tier limit, or returns on a second day. ProductLed’s benchmark article reports higher free-account conversion among companies using PQLs, while activation measurement is often underused.[2] This is why Lumae should instrument activation before spending significantly on acquisition.

> **Initial activation definition:** A user creates one channel-ready item and takes one persistence action—save, copy, repurpose, schedule, or export—during the same session.

## 7. 90-Day Execution Plan

### Days 1–30: Establish Clarity and Instrumentation

The first month is about message consistency and learning. Unify the free-tier promise across home, pricing, onboarding, and social profiles. Create one landing page for the initial audience segment, such as “Lumae for solo service creators.” Add event tracking for landing-page CTA, account start, confirmed account, first generation, first saved output, return visit, upgrade click, and paid conversion.

Publish 12–16 founder-led short demonstrations and four LinkedIn lessons. Release the first starter template and invite 10–15 target users to try the product in exchange for structured feedback—not testimonials. Hold five short user conversations focused on where they get stuck between sign-up and first useful output.

### Days 31–60: Build Repeatable Distribution and Proof

Turn the best-performing first-month themes into a series. Begin the micro-creator pilot, running 5–8 small partnerships with unique links and one shared content brief. Publish two use-case pages: one for solo creators and one for small businesses. Add two Journal articles that answer high-intent questions, such as “How to turn one idea into a week of content” and “A simple content workflow for service businesses.”

Create an onboarding email sequence only after email delivery and confirmation are functioning reliably for the relevant account type. The sequence should be behavior-driven: welcome after confirmation, first-output prompt if inactive, save/repurpose prompt after generation, and upgrade explanation only after a genuine usage trigger.

### Days 61–90: Improve Conversion and Retention

Compare content hooks, target-segment pages, and creator-partner cohorts. Keep only the channels that create activated users, not merely impressions. Add an in-product upgrade prompt tied to the user’s actual value moment, such as saving a workflow, reaching a usage limit, or attempting scheduling/analytics. Publish one customer-style workflow story only with the user’s explicit permission and truthful attribution.

At day 90, choose one scale decision: deepen the strongest organic channel, expand the best partner program, or test a small paid retargeting campaign. Do not spread budget across all channels before an activation signal is proven.

## 8. Weekly Operating Rhythm

| Day | Activity | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Review prior-week funnel and content data. | One-page weekly growth scorecard and one experiment decision. |
| Tuesday | Create a focused workflow demonstration. | One Reel/Short, one carousel, one LinkedIn adaptation. |
| Wednesday | Speak to users or prospects. | At least one insight added to a message/objection log. |
| Thursday | Publish an educational or comparison asset. | Journal post, template, or product walkthrough. |
| Friday | Partner and community outreach. | Five relevant creator/community conversations, personalized and non-spammy. |

## 9. Measurement Framework

Do not use reach as the primary success measure. Track reach for awareness, but make decisions based on progression toward activated use.

| Metric | Definition | First 90-day use |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified visitor | A visitor from a targeted source who reaches a use-case or pricing CTA. | Judge message/channel fit. |
| Visitor-to-account rate | Accounts started or created ÷ qualified visitors. | Judge landing-page clarity. |
| Confirmation completion | Confirmed email/password accounts ÷ local accounts created. | Identify trust and delivery friction. |
| Activation rate | Users completing the defined first value action ÷ confirmed/new users. | Primary product-marketing metric. |
| D7 return rate | Activated users active again within seven days ÷ activated users. | Judge repeatable value. |
| PQL rate | Users meeting Lumae’s PQL definition ÷ free users. | Identify users ready for upgrade attention. |
| Free-to-paid conversion | New paid customers ÷ eligible free users. | Evaluate monetization, not vanity. |
| Partner activation rate | Activated users from a partner ÷ partner-driven account starts. | Decide whether to repeat a partnership. |

Use external benchmarks only as context. ProductLed cites a 12% median visitor-to-freemium conversion rate and an approximately 9% free-to-paid conversion rate across models, but Lumae’s audience, price point, geography, and free offer may produce different results.[2] Establish a clean internal baseline in the first 30 days before committing to numerical targets.

## 10. Immediate Seven-Day Checklist

| Priority | Owner | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Founder/Product | Resolve the public free-tier promise conflict and update every customer-facing surface. |
| 2 | Founder | Choose the primary audience: solo service creators is recommended. |
| 3 | Product | Confirm the activation event and add a simple funnel dashboard. |
| 4 | Marketing | Produce three workflow demonstrations from one real creator problem. |
| 5 | Marketing | Publish one use-case landing section and one starter template. |
| 6 | Founder | Invite 10–15 target users for feedback sessions; ask about workflow pain, not for praise. |
| 7 | Marketing | Build a message/objection log from comments, calls, and support questions. |

## 11. What Not To Do Yet

Do not invest heavily in broad paid ads before Lumae measures activation. Do not claim that Lumae guarantees virality, replaces human judgment, or sends an email when a provider has not accepted it. Do not create fictitious customer reviews, results, ratings, or case studies. These shortcuts damage the trust that Lumae needs for durable growth.
